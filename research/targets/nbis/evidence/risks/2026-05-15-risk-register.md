## High-Priority Risks

| Risk | Why it matters | Evidence anchor | Monitor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Financing scale | 2026 capex expectations are far larger than current quarterly revenue. | Q1 capex was about $2.5B; call transcript indicates 2026 capex guidance of $20B-$25B. | Debt terms, asset-backed financing, customer prepayments, ATM/equity issuance, dilution. |
| Delivery execution | Microsoft/Meta contracts require large capacity to arrive on schedule. | Microsoft 6-K includes termination rights tied to missed delivery dates after grace periods. | Tranche go-live dates, service-level credits, construction delays, alternative capacity. |
| NVIDIA dependence | GPU supply and economics are the core input. | 20-F says Nebius currently relies on NVIDIA for GPU chips. | GPU allocation, Blackwell/Rubin timing, price changes, export controls, supplier diversification. |
| Power and sites | AI factories need large, reliable, permitted power. | Q1 release announced up to 1.2GW Pennsylvania site; 20-F highlights power/grid constraints. | Interconnection approvals, utility agreements, local opposition, ratepayer/regulatory conditions. |
| Customer concentration | A few large contracts can dominate revenue visibility and bargaining power. | Microsoft and Meta agreements are multi-billion dollar commitments. | Revenue by customer, receivables, deferred revenue, RPO, renewal/pricing terms. |
| Utilization and price compression | Non-dedicated capacity must be filled at attractive rates. | 20-F warns about underutilized capacity and pricing pressure. | Utilization, spot/on-demand pricing, contract duration, gross margin. |
| Technology demand shift | More efficient models or custom silicon could reduce GPU cloud demand. | 20-F warns that model/inference efficiency can reduce or change demand. | Tokens per GPU economics, enterprise inference mix, TPU/Trainium/Maia adoption. |
| Balance-sheet duration mismatch | GPU assets depreciate quickly while debt and contracts run over multi-year terms. | Q1 balance sheet shows non-current debt growth to $8.432B. | Debt maturities, interest expense, depreciation, residual GPU value, refresh capex. |

## Base-Case Risk View

The biggest risk is not whether AI compute demand exists today. The evidence already shows named demand. The bigger question is whether the economics of that demand stay attractive after financing costs, GPU depreciation, construction delays, customer bargaining power, and future price normalization.

For AIFi tracking, NBIS should be treated as a high-beta execution compounder. It can look financially strong when upfront payments and strategic contracts arrive, while still carrying substantial future delivery and refinancing obligations.
