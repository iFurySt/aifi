## Capacity and Guidance Signals

- Nebius announced that it secured up to 1.2GW of power and land for a new owned AI factory in Pennsylvania.
- The shareholder letter says Nebius expects 800MW to 1GW of connected power by year-end 2026.
- Management commentary in the Q1 call, as captured by a public transcript provider, says 2026 capex expectations were raised to $20B-$25B from $16B-$20B.
- The same transcript source says management reiterated 2026 group revenue guidance of $3.0B-$3.4B, year-end ARR guidance of $7B-$9B, and group adjusted EBITDA margin around 40%.

## SEC Risk Baseline

The 2025 Form 20-F frames Nebius as a business with unusually high dependence on external inputs:

- Supplier concentration: Nebius says it currently relies on NVIDIA for the GPU chips it uses and on a limited number of suppliers for other key infrastructure components.
- Data-center execution: future growth relies on significant expansion of the data-center footprint, including sites, build-to-suit or colocation arrangements, reliable power, and construction timelines.
- Power and grid constraints: Nebius depends on third-party power, network, internet, and water inputs; AI data-center demand is increasing scrutiny around grid reliability and ratepayer impact.
- Customer contract execution: long-term customer arrangements can create cost overruns, underutilized capacity, margin pressure, delayed revenue recognition, disputes, or early termination.
- Demand uncertainty: model and inference efficiency improvements could change or reduce demand for GPU-heavy AI services.
- Financing risk: scale requires continuing access to capital, including debt, asset-backed financing, prepayments, and potentially equity.

## Investment Read-Through

Nebius is not a speculative chip-design story; it is a fast-scaling AI infrastructure operating story. The evidence points to real customer demand and a meaningful margin bridge in the AI cloud segment, but the core diligence issue is whether the company can finance, build, power, fill, and refresh GPU capacity fast enough without giving away too much economics to customers, lenders, suppliers, or shareholders.
