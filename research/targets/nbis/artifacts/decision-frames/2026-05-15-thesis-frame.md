# Nebius Investment Thesis Frame

## One-Line View

Nebius is a levered, execution-heavy way to own NVIDIA-era AI infrastructure demand. It is a more commercially validated near-term story than Cerebras, but probably has a weaker architectural moat because it rides the NVIDIA stack rather than replacing it.

## What Matters Most

Nebius is not trying to beat NVIDIA. It is trying to build and operate AI factories fast enough that Microsoft, Meta, startups, and enterprises prefer renting capacity from Nebius instead of waiting for internal hyperscaler capacity or building it themselves.

That makes the company a test of three things:

1. Demand visibility: Microsoft, Meta, and AI cloud customers need to keep absorbing capacity at attractive prices.
2. Supply execution: Nebius needs GPUs, power, sites, construction, networking, software, and operations to arrive on schedule.
3. Capital structure: the company needs financing that does not transfer too much upside to lenders, customers, suppliers, or new shareholders.

## Bull Case

- Microsoft, Meta, and NVIDIA validate that Nebius is not a marginal GPU renter.
- Customer prepayments and asset-backed financing can turn long-term contracts into a repeatable infrastructure flywheel.
- The AI cloud segment already shows strong adjusted EBITDA margin before the full scale-up.
- NVIDIA partnership may improve access to latest systems, architecture support, fleet management, and customer credibility.
- Token Factory plus Eigen AI / Clarifai / Tavily can move Nebius above commodity GPU leasing if they create managed inference, agentic AI, and platform stickiness.

## Bear Case

- Capex is enormous relative to current revenue and requires near-continuous financing.
- Customer contracts create obligations before the company proves multi-year operating reliability at gigawatt scale.
- NVIDIA dependence limits negotiating power and creates single-supplier exposure.
- If GPU supply normalizes or hyperscalers internalize capacity, pricing could compress.
- The company could appear profitable on adjusted EBITDA while depreciation, interest, dilution, and refresh capex consume the economics.
- Legacy restructuring, non-core assets, and fast acquisitions add complexity around a business that already has execution risk.

## Current AIFi Stance

Nebius deserves active coverage because it sits directly in the "AI inference / AI cloud third platform" discussion: after hyperscalers and NVIDIA's own ecosystem, specialized AI clouds like Nebius and CoreWeave are becoming a separate capacity layer.

Initial stance: constructive but not casual. The business has unusually strong customer validation, but the underwriting work should focus on financing quality, delivery milestones, and normalized economics rather than headline contract value.

## Upgrade Triggers

- Verified 2026 connected power and active GPU capacity ramps without major delay.
- More detail showing customer prepayments and asset-backed debt cover most incremental capex.
- Sustained AI cloud adjusted EBITDA margin while depreciation and interest expense rise.
- Customer diversification beyond Microsoft/Meta and large AI labs.
- Evidence that Token Factory / Eigen AI improves inference margins or retention.

## Downgrade Triggers

- Delivery delays or service-level issues on Microsoft or Meta capacity.
- Equity issuance or debt terms that imply weak financing options.
- Falling utilization or visible GPU cloud price pressure.
- NVIDIA supply delays or worse-than-expected terms.
- Rising capex without matching contracted revenue visibility.
- Adjusted EBITDA diverges materially from free cash flow and owner earnings.
