## Business Model

Cerebras is positioning as an AI infrastructure company rather than a standalone chip vendor. The stack includes:

- WSE wafer-scale processor.
- CS-3 integrated system with power, cooling, and interconnect.
- Cerebras AI supercomputers formed from multiple CS-3 systems.
- Software for training and inference without customers directly managing distributed GPU infrastructure.
- Cloud, partner cloud, on-premises, hybrid deployment, and AI model services.

The company says WSE-3 has 4T transistors, 900,000 compute cores, 44 GB on-chip memory, and 21 PB/s memory bandwidth. It also claims inference can be up to 15x faster than leading GPU-based systems on leading open-source models.

## Customer Concentration

- G42 accounted for 24.0% of 2025 revenue and 85.0% of 2024 revenue.
- MBZUAI accounted for 62.0% of 2025 revenue.
- MBZUAI accounted for 77.9% of accounts receivable at 2025 year-end.
- G42 accounted for 91.0% of accounts receivable at 2024 year-end.
- G42 and MBZUAI are related parties under ASC 850.

Interpretation: 2025 growth was not yet diversified. The headline customer list now includes OpenAI and AWS, but the filed revenue base was still dominated by two UAE-linked counterparties.

## OpenAI And RPO

- In January 2026 Cerebras announced a multi-year OpenAI deal valued at more than $20B.
- OpenAI agreed to deploy 750 MW of Cerebras high-speed AI compute and co-design future models for future Cerebras hardware.
- Remaining performance obligations were $24.6B as of 2025-12-31.
- A significant amount of RPO was attributable to the OpenAI master relationship agreement.
- Expected RPO recognition: about 15% through 2027, 43% during months 25-48, and the rest thereafter.
- OpenAI provided a $1.0B working-capital loan, maturing no later than 2032 and bearing 6% annual interest unless waived under specified circumstances.

Interpretation: OpenAI is the core upside and the core execution risk. The RPO number is large relative to 2025 revenue, but revenue recognition depends on capacity delivery, data-center readiness, power, service levels, and contract terms.

## AWS Relationship

- In March 2026 Cerebras signed a binding term sheet with AWS.
- AWS is expected to be the first hyperscaler to deploy Cerebras in AWS data centers.
- Binding terms include pricing, exclusivity, minimum capacity, and an initial multi-year lease.
- The term sheet includes a warrant commitment for up to 2,696,678 Class N shares at a $100 exercise price, with vesting tied to product purchases well beyond the initial lease and other triggers.
- Definitive agreements still need to be negotiated and executed.

Interpretation: AWS is a strong distribution signal but not yet the same as fully de-risked recurring revenue.

## Key Filing Risks

- Concentrated customer base and related-party exposure.
- MRA delivery deadlines and service-level commitments with OpenAI.
- Data-center capacity, power availability, lease obligations, and provider disputes.
- Need for significant additional capital to scale cloud offerings.
- Hardware revenue lumpiness while cloud revenue is early.
- Competition from NVIDIA, AMD, custom silicon, and hyperscaler internal chips.
- Export controls and other government regulation.
- Third-party and sole-source supplier dependence, with many services/components bought by purchase order without capacity or volume commitments.
- Identified material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting.

## Analyst Takeaway

Cerebras has unusually strong proof points for demand visibility after the OpenAI agreement, but the company is moving from a hardware-system sales model into a capital-intensive AI cloud / data-center model. The central diligence question is not whether demand exists; it is whether Cerebras can turn very large contract commitments into reliable, profitable capacity faster than NVIDIA and hyperscaler-owned silicon compress the opportunity.
