## One-Line View

Cerebras is a high-upside AI infrastructure scarcity asset with unusually large OpenAI-backed demand visibility, but at the May 2026 IPO-debut valuation the stock already prices in near-flawless execution across data-center buildout, customer diversification, and durable speed advantage versus NVIDIA and hyperscaler-owned silicon.

## What The Business Is

Cerebras sells a full-stack AI compute platform built around the Wafer-Scale Engine. Instead of connecting many smaller GPUs, it puts extreme compute, on-chip memory, and bandwidth on a wafer-scale processor inside CS-3 systems, then wraps that in software, cloud access, partner clouds, on-prem deployments, and model services.

The practical pitch is speed and simplicity: faster inference, fewer distributed-system headaches, and a differentiated architecture for customers that care about latency-sensitive AI applications such as coding agents, reasoning workloads, research agents, and real-time applications.

## Bull Case

- Product differentiation is real enough to win OpenAI and AWS attention.
- OpenAI agreement gives more than $20B headline contract value and drives a large $24.6B remaining performance obligation base.
- AI inference demand may grow faster than training demand, and Cerebras is deliberately positioned around fast inference rather than only model training.
- AWS could turn Cerebras from a niche high-performance architecture into a broadly available cloud option.
- The company raised over $5.4B of IPO proceeds before other offering expenses, giving it capital to build data-center capacity.

## Bear Case

- Revenue concentration is extreme: MBZUAI and G42 together represented 86% of 2025 revenue, and they are related parties.
- GAAP 2025 net income is misleading as a core-profit signal; non-GAAP net loss and operating loss remained negative.
- OpenAI is both the largest validation point and a major single-contract execution dependency.
- The move from hardware-system sales to cloud/data-center services is capital intensive and operationally hard.
- NVIDIA's ecosystem, hyperscaler custom silicon, and future ASICs can compress the opportunity before Cerebras proves durable margins.
- The IPO pop makes valuation fragile: if execution evidence is merely good rather than exceptional, multiple compression can dominate fundamentals.

## What Must Be True For The Stock To Work

1. Cerebras must deliver OpenAI capacity on time and with acceptable service levels.
2. AWS must progress from binding term sheet to real deployment, marketplace usage, and follow-on commitments.
3. Revenue must diversify so the story is not only OpenAI plus UAE-linked counterparties.
4. Cloud gross margins must become attractive despite power, leases, hardware depreciation, and pass-through costs.
5. The WSE architecture must keep a latency/cost advantage after NVIDIA, AMD, and custom silicon responses.
6. Internal controls and public-company reporting must improve quickly enough to avoid credibility discounts.

## Current Rating Language

For a watchlist, Cerebras belongs in the "high potential, high verification burden" bucket.

At or near the first-day post-IPO price, the risk/reward looks asymmetric unless an investor has a differentiated view that OpenAI/AWS deployments will turn into rapid, profitable, diversified revenue. The right posture is not "avoid because risky"; it is "demand evidence before paying a fully de-risked multiple."

## Triggers To Upgrade

- First public quarter shows clean revenue growth from customers beyond MBZUAI/G42.
- Management quantifies OpenAI capacity delivery progress and gross margin profile.
- AWS definitive agreement is signed and commercial availability expands.
- Non-GAAP operating loss narrows while RPO conversion accelerates.
- Independent benchmarks show durable speed/cost advantage on commercially important workloads.

## Triggers To Downgrade

- OpenAI delivery delays, service credits, or contract renegotiation.
- AWS definitive agreement stalls or becomes narrow.
- Revenue remains dominated by MBZUAI/G42/OpenAI without broader enterprise adoption.
- Data-center lease/power costs push cloud gross margin below expectations.
- New NVIDIA or hyperscaler silicon closes the latency gap.
- Material weakness remediation slips or reporting quality becomes an investor issue.

## Open Questions

- What percentage of the $24.6B RPO is pass-through data-center cost versus high-margin compute revenue?
- What is the unit economics of a deployed MW of Cerebras capacity?
- How much of OpenAI usage is production-critical versus workload-specific?
- What is normalized gross margin when cloud revenue becomes a larger mix?
- How much revenue can AWS generate before warrant dilution and exclusivity tradeoffs matter?
- How quickly can Cerebras develop a repeatable enterprise sales motion outside the largest strategic accounts?
