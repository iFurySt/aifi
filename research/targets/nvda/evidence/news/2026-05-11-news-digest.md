## Demand Signals

- Microsoft said calendar 2026 capex is expected to be roughly $190B, including about $25B from higher component pricing, and that it expects to remain capacity-constrained at least through 2026.
- Microsoft also said roughly two-thirds of Q3 FY2026 capex was for short-lived assets, primarily GPUs and CPUs.
- Alphabet reported Q1 2026 Google Cloud revenue of $20.028B, up from $12.260B in Q1 2025, and trailing-twelve-month purchases of property and equipment of $109.924B.
- Amazon reported Q1 2026 purchases of property and equipment of $44.203B and said it would deploy one million-plus NVIDIA GPUs starting in 2026, while also landing more than 2.1M AI chips over the prior 12 months, more than half of which were Trainium.

## Narrative Changes

- Confirmed: NVIDIA remains the main monetization point for AI infrastructure buildout.
- Confirmed: The investment case has shifted from GPU share alone to full-stack systems: compute, networking, software, reference architectures, and AI factory deployment.
- Confirmed: Hyperscalers still need NVIDIA even while investing heavily in custom silicon.
- Unresolved: Whether China revenue returns through H200 licenses, remains blocked, or structurally shifts to domestic Chinese accelerators.
- Unresolved: Whether hyperscaler AI capex produces enough revenue/ROI to sustain 2026-2027 purchasing intensity.
