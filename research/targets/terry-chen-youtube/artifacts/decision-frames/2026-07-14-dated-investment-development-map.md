## Development Thesis

Terry's path is better modeled as a sequence of capabilities than as one portfolio to copy:

```text
skill income -> investable surplus -> concentrated upside -> operating assets -> yield/risk experiments -> liquidity and income failovers -> salary-independent optionality
```

The practical lesson for ordinary investors is not to copy the final asset weights. The transferable part is the order of operations: build surplus, make a simple core, use small experiments to learn, separate advanced modules by risk, and only scale a module after its failure modes are understood.

## Practical Portfolio Template Derived From The Map

For an ordinary investor, the clean translation is a staged policy:

| Stage | Portfolio action | Scaling gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Runway | Hold 3-12 months of expenses in cash-like instruments before scaling risky assets. | You can cover job loss or emergency without selling equities. |
| 2. Core | Automate a broad-market ETF or target-date-style allocation as the default engine. | You can explain why this beats most active attempts after behavior costs. |
| 3. Learning satellite | Use 0-10% of risky assets for one-stock or thematic research. | Each position has thesis, max size, review date, and failure rule. |
| 4. Stabilizer | Add Treasuries, high-quality bonds, or cash equivalents based on age, income stability, and drawdown tolerance. | You know why your stabilizer may fail in inflation or rate-shock regimes. |
| 5. Operating assets | Add rental, business, or creator projects only after modeling net economics. | Conservative case still works after vacancies, fees, taxes, repairs, and time cost. |
| 6. Options income | Paper-trade before using real capital; keep it tied to holdings you are willing to own. | You understand assignment, rolling, tax, and missed-upside risk. |
| 7. Crypto / neutral assets | Keep a capped sleeve and separate BTC/ETH ownership from platform/stablecoin yield. | You can tolerate a deep drawdown or total platform loss without lifestyle damage. |

## Practice-First Learning Plan

1. Personal cash-flow audit: 90 days of income, expenses, taxes, debt, savings, and runway.
2. Baseline portfolio simulation: compare monthly DCA, lump sum, and 30-50% drawdown outcomes.
3. One-stock drill: write bull case, bear case, valuation range, max size, and what would prove the thesis wrong.
4. Real-estate underwriting: model rent, vacancy, repairs, insurance, taxes, manager fees, financing, legal structure, and exit value.
5. Options lab: paper-trade covered-call or cash-secured-put cycles before using real holdings.
6. Yield risk checklist: identify who pays the yield, what collateral exists, how liquidation works, who custodies assets, and what happens if withdrawals stop.
7. Quarterly policy review: rebalance ranges, max single-name exposure, liquidity runway, and failure conditions for each income engine.
