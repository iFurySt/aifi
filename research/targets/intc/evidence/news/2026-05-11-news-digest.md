## Narrative Changes

- The market narrative shifted from "Intel is a broken legacy chipmaker" to "Intel may be a scarce US advanced-manufacturing asset with AI CPU demand and external foundry call options."
- The strongest confirmed evidence is not Apple; it is Q1 execution: revenue above guidance, DCAI growth, non-GAAP margin improvement, and better supply/yield commentary.
- Apple and Terafab are upside options. They are important because external customers are exactly what Intel needs to justify 14A/foundry economics, but they are not yet fully bankable revenue streams.

## Confirmed Facts

- Intel's Q1 2026 revenue was $13.6B, up 7% YoY, and Q2 revenue guidance was $13.8B-$14.8B.
- NVIDIA's 2025 collaboration included custom Intel x86 CPUs for NVIDIA AI infrastructure and a planned $5B Intel equity investment.
- The U.S. government investment was structured as passive ownership with no board seat, and a warrant tied to Intel's foundry ownership falling below 51%.
- SoftBank agreed to invest $2B in Intel common stock at $23/share.

## Unconfirmed Or Disputed Claims

- Apple: Bloomberg described exploratory talks on May 5; later market reports cited a WSJ preliminary agreement on May 8. The retrieved sources do not include an Intel or Apple confirmation.
- Terafab: Reuters/Investing.com reported Musk's plan to use Intel 14A, but timelines, committed volumes, economics, and risk-sharing are still not clear.
