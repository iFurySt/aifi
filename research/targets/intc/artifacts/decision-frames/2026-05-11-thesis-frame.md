## Executive Snapshot

Intel's rally is not random. It is a forced re-rating from three things happening at once:

1. Q1 2026 showed real operating improvement in products and DCAI.
2. AI inference/agentic workloads revived the CPU demand story.
3. Foundry optionality suddenly became more credible through 18A/14A progress, Terafab, Apple reports, and policy-backed US manufacturing scarcity.

The problem is price. At about $125/share and roughly $635B market cap, Intel is no longer a cheap distressed turnaround. It is being priced as if the turnaround is materially de-risked. The asset is now a high-variance strategic-option stock.

## Bull Case

- Core claim: Intel becomes the strategic US foundry/advanced-packaging platform while its x86 CPU franchise participates in AI inference demand.
- Evidence: DCAI +22% YoY, 18A yield commentary, Google/NVIDIA relationships, Terafab 14A report, Apple manufacturing reports, US government/NVIDIA/SoftBank capital support.
- What would confirm it:
  - Signed external 14A/18A customer commitments with volume, timing, and economics.
  - Foundry loss narrows every quarter without hiding behind accounting adjustments.
  - DCAI keeps double-digit growth after supply constraints ease.
  - Adjusted FCF turns positive even with capex.
- What would break it:
  - Apple/Terafab remain headlines without material production commitments.
  - 18A ramp pressures gross margin more than management expects.
  - 14A slips or lacks a major external customer.

## Base Case

- Core claim: Intel is improving, but the stock has front-loaded much of the improvement; future returns depend on execution milestones rather than broad narrative.
- Evidence: Q1 beat was real, but Q2 EPS guide is lower than Q1, adjusted FCF is still negative, Foundry loss is still large, and PC demand is expected to weaken in 2H 2026.
- What would confirm it:
  - Q2/Q3 meet guidance, Foundry losses narrow, but external customer announcements remain early-stage.
  - Stock becomes volatile and milestone-driven rather than compounding smoothly.
- What would break it:
  - A confirmed Apple or other major foundry deal with credible economics.
  - A Q2 miss or margin disappointment.

## Bear Case

- Core claim: The market is overpaying for optionality before economics are proven.
- Evidence: Market cap around $635B versus Q1 non-GAAP net income of $1.485B, GAAP loss, negative FCF, $45B debt before the post-quarter $6.5B Fab 34 loan, and large Foundry losses.
- What would confirm it:
  - No company-confirmed Apple deal.
  - Foundry external revenue remains small.
  - Gross margin guide steps down further as 18A ramps.
  - Capital markets penalize the balance sheet or refinancing.
- What would break it:
  - Signed large external foundry orders with credible margins and prepayments.

## Contradictions And Unresolved Questions

- Intel is supply constrained, which supports pricing, but supply constraints can mask true demand quality.
- Q1 non-GAAP improvement is real, but GAAP and FCF remain weak.
- Apple/Terafab are strategically powerful, but the retrieved sources do not yet prove signed high-volume economics.
- The stock may still look cheap versus AI winners on sales multiples, but it is expensive versus current Intel earnings and cash flow.

## Working View

Intel is investable only as a measured, high-variance turnaround/strategic-option sleeve, not as a clean compounder. The business evidence improved enough that "dead money" is no longer the right frame. But after the rally, the stock requires proof, not stories.

Practical stance: do not chase it as a full-sized allocation after a 100%+ monthly move. If already owned, treat it as a position that needs active risk management and milestone-based trimming/adds. If not owned, wait for either a reset in price or company-confirmed evidence that external foundry demand is real and economic.
