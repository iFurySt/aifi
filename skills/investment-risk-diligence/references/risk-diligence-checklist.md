# Risk Diligence Checklist

Use this checklist to turn risk review into an evidence-backed diligence note,
not a generic list of things that could go wrong.

## Risk Categories

| Category | Questions to ask | Common evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business model | What can impair growth, pricing, retention, or unit economics? | Filings, segment data, customer commentary, product updates. |
| Competition | Where can peers, substitutes, or customers erode returns? | Peer results, product roadmaps, market share data. |
| Financial quality | Are revenue, margins, cash flow, debt, and working capital sustainable? | Financial snapshot, filings, earnings releases. |
| Accounting quality | Are adjustments, accruals, revenue recognition, inventory, impairments, or reserves concerning? | 10-K/10-Q notes, auditor changes, non-GAAP reconciliations. |
| Liquidity and financing | Can the company fund operations, debt maturities, capex, dividends, buybacks, or acquisitions? | Debt footnotes, cash flow, credit ratings, maturity schedules. |
| Governance | Do incentives, control structure, board quality, related-party transactions, or capital allocation create risk? | Proxy filings, governance policies, insider transactions. |
| Legal and regulatory | Could investigations, litigation, sanctions, approvals, or policy changes alter value? | Filings, court records, regulator releases, reputable news. |
| Macro and cycle | How exposed is the target to rates, FX, commodities, demand cycles, or fiscal policy? | Market data, segment data, macro-sensitive disclosures. |
| Operational execution | What must management deliver, and what evidence shows slippage? | Earnings Q&A, guidance history, project milestones. |
| Market and positioning | Is the security crowded, over-owned, illiquid, heavily shorted, or dependent on sentiment? | Market signal analysis, ownership, short interest, options data. |

## Review Rules

- Start with risks that are specific to the company or security. Generic market
  risks belong lower unless they are central to the thesis.
- Compare company risk-factor language against recent events and financial
  trends. A risk that moved from hypothetical to observed should be elevated.
- Treat management explanations as evidence, not as proof.
- Use trend direction when exact probability is unknowable.
- Pair every material risk with the best available confirming and disconfirming
  evidence.
- Identify what would make the risk no longer material.

## Red Flags

Escalate these for manual review when present:

- auditor resignation, restatement, material weakness, or unexplained filing
  delay
- large non-GAAP adjustments with weak reconciliation
- rising receivables, inventory, capitalized costs, or impairments inconsistent
  with the business narrative
- covenant pressure, near-term maturities, refinancing dependence, or going
  concern language
- concentrated customer, supplier, country, or product exposure without clear
  mitigation
- management turnover, related-party transactions, dual-class control issues, or
  unusual insider activity
- regulatory investigation, sanctions exposure, major litigation, or dependency
  on subsidies and approvals

## Output Discipline

- Use "risk may be rising" only when there is evidence of trend change.
- Avoid assigning precise probabilities unless the source supports them.
- State when risk assessment is limited by missing filings, private-company
  data, stale market data, or inaccessible legal records.
